# Directing like the directors: a craft reference for image-to-video prompts

How to read this file:

- Fields 1–9 describe the real craft. Field 10 is the only part meant to be pasted into a prompt; it never names a director or film.
- Speeds (m/s, degrees per second) and frame rates in field 10 are prompt conventions, not production records.
- "(unverified)" marks a claim the web check for this file did not confirm. Treat it as a working assumption.
- Focal lengths are given for the format used, with a Super 35 equivalent where the format is large.
- Field 9 is sound effects only. No music anywhere.

---

## Stanley Kubrick

- **1. Signature:** A dead-centre, symmetrical corridor seen through a very wide lens, with the camera gliding straight down the vanishing line.
- **2. Lens and format:** Wide spherical primes. An 18mm was the workhorse on The Shining, and American Cinematographer reports most of Eyes Wide Shut was shot on a Zeiss 18mm, rarely going longer than 35mm. Deep focus by default. The exception is the candlelit work on Barry Lyndon, shot on a Zeiss Planar 50mm f/0.7, which gives paper-thin focus. Aspect ratios around 1.66–1.85 (unverified); 2.20 on 65mm for 2001 (unverified).
- **3. Camera:** Tripod, dolly and Steadicam. Steadicam follows from behind or leads from the front at walking pace, often low to the floor (low height unverified). Straight-line moves only: push in, pull back, lateral track.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** One-point perspective, subject on the centre line, architecture converging behind them. Sets are built to be shown, so the frame holds floor and ceiling. Actors walk along the lens axis, towards or away from camera.
- **5. Light:** Practicals and source light that is visible in frame: windows, overhead fluorescents, chandeliers, candles. Hard or flat rather than flattering.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Clean, neutral base with one saturated accent, often red. Cold white institutional interiors; warm amber for candlelight.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Long holds. Movement is slow and constant. Action arrives late in the shot, after the audience has had time to read the room.
- **8. Performance:** Still bodies, slow delivery, then a sudden spike. The lowered-chin, eyes-up stare into the lens.
- **9. SFX signature:** Empty room tone, long reverb tails, footsteps and wheels changing surface (hard floor to carpet), mechanical hum, breathing inside a helmet.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "locked symmetrical one-point perspective, subject dead centre, vanishing point at eye level"
  - "18mm-equivalent wide spherical lens, deep focus, floor and ceiling both visible"
  - "slow, perfectly straight Steadicam push down the corridor at 0.3 m/s, no sway, no pan"
  - "subject motionless, chin lowered, eyes raised to the lens"
  - "SFX: hollow room tone, distant ventilation hum, single footsteps with long reverb"
- **11. Avoid:** Handheld shake. Shallow-focus portrait look (unless the candlelight variant). Off-centre, casual framing. Fast cuts.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for architecture, corridors, product on a plinth, dread, formal reveals. Worst for warm comedy, loose lifestyle, fast action.

## Denis Villeneuve (with Roger Deakins and Greig Fraser)

- **1. Signature:** A tiny human silhouette against a huge, simple shape in haze, held in near silence.
- **2. Lens and format:** Mid-range spherical primes with Deakins: Alexa XT Studio and Zeiss Master Primes on Blade Runner 2049, with a stated preference for roughly 27–50mm (range reported, not confirmed film by film). With Fraser, Dune used the Alexa LF: spherical Panavision H Series for 1.43 IMAX scenes and Ultra Vista 1.65x anamorphics for 2.39 scenes. Dune: Part Two was all spherical on Alexa 65 and Mini LF. Moderate depth of field, clean glass.
- **3. Camera:** Tripod, dolly, crane, stabilised head. Slow, weighty moves: creeping push-in, slow crane rise, slow aerial drift.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Extreme scale contrast. Large empty areas of frame. Silhouettes and profiles. Simple geometry: one horizon, one monolith, one figure. Actors move slowly through space, often small in frame.
- **5. Light:** One big motivated source. Soft, directional, wrapped in atmosphere: dust, fog, smoke, rain. Deep shadow with detail.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Near-monochrome scenes: all amber, all cyan-grey, all sand. Low saturation, one hue per location.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Long, quiet holds, then a sudden loud event. Movement is slow and inevitable.
- **8. Performance:** Internal. Faces held still, listening, breathing. Little dialogue.
- **9. SFX signature:** Sub-bass rumble, wind, sand hiss, distant mechanical groans, muffled impacts, and deliberate drops to near silence before a hit.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "extreme wide shot, lone figure very small against a massive simple structure, thick atmospheric haze"
  - "single soft directional light source, subject in silhouette, deep shadows with detail"
  - "monochrome palette, everything in desaturated amber" (or "cold cyan-grey")
  - "very slow push-in, 0.1 m/s, camera perfectly stable"
  - "SFX: low sub-bass rumble, steady wind, then near silence"
- **11. Avoid:** Busy frames. Multiple colour accents. Whip pans or handheld jitter. Bright, even commercial lighting.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for scale, landscape, vehicles, reveals, awe, premium tech. Worst for comedy, rapid dialogue, cheerful lifestyle.

## Christopher Nolan

- **1. Signature:** Large-format, practical, in-camera spectacle with a tall frame and a face close to a big, heavy camera.
- **2. Lens and format:** 15-perf IMAX (1.43:1) intercut with 5-perf 65mm (2.20:1), per Kodak. Hoyte van Hoytema's go-to IMAX lenses were 50mm and 80mm, roughly 24mm and 40mm in Super 35 terms; a custom 40mm was built for close-ups on Oppenheimer. Spherical on large format. Earlier films used 35mm anamorphic at 2.39 (unverified). Photochemical finishing.
- **3. Camera:** IMAX handheld on the shoulder, hard mounts on aircraft and vehicles, crane, dolly. Moves are functional: follow, track alongside, hold on a face.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Centre-weighted, classical framing. Close-ups on large format, so a face fills the frame while the background stays readable.
- **5. Light:** Naturalistic. Daylight, overcast skies, practicals. Low fill. Faces often lit from the side or by a window.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Film-print look. Cool blue-steel and warm skin, natural saturation, real black.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Each shot carries urgent, continuous action. Shots are medium length, built to be cross-cut.
- **8. Performance:** Tense, controlled, purposeful. Men in suits or gear solving a problem under pressure.
- **9. SFX signature:** Ticking, engine drone, pressure hiss, very loud realistic gunshots and impacts, and total silence for a vacuum or just before a blast.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "shot on 70mm large-format film, tall 1.43:1 frame, fine grain, extreme detail"
  - "24mm-equivalent spherical lens close to the face, background still sharp enough to read"
  - "handheld large-format camera, slight natural weight in the movement"
  - "SFX: mechanical ticking, low engine drone, sharp realistic impact"
- **11. Avoid:** Obvious CG gloss. Heavy teal-orange grade. Slow dreamy drifting. Soft, flared, vintage glass.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for vehicles, aviation, practical action, scale with a human face. Worst for whimsy, soft romance, stylised colour.

## David Fincher

- **1. Signature:** A dark, immaculate frame where the camera moves exactly as much as the actor does, and no more.
- **2. Lens and format:** Digital since The Social Network, on RED cameras. Spherical primes (Leitz Summilux-C on The Killer). 2.39 or wider framing (unverified). Captured larger than the frame so shots can be reframed and stabilised in post. Moderate to shallow depth of field in low light.
- **3. Camera:** Tripod and dolly. Almost no handheld; where it appears it is a deliberate effect. Moves are motivated: the camera tilts as someone sits, pans as they cross, pushes in imperceptibly when a thought lands. Speed tracks the character's speed.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Clean, level, balanced. Eye-level or slightly below. Information is placed precisely; nothing accidental in frame.
- **5. Light:** Motivated by practicals and windows. Low-key, soft, top or side. Dark, but without crushed blacks.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Narrow palette. Cold blue-grey against sodium yellow; warm orange can replace blue at night. Low saturation. A green-yellow cast in interiors (unverified).
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Precise. Short to medium shots, each a single clean action. The move starts and ends exactly with the actor.
- **8. Performance:** Fast, flat, under-played. Micro-expressions. People who are hiding something.
- **9. SFX signature:** Dense, low room tone; fluorescent buzz; rain on glass; keyboard clicks; a pen on paper; the small close sound of a clean, clinical action.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "camera movement locked to the subject: it tilts and pans only as the subject moves, same speed, then stops with them"
  - "low-key interior lit only by practical lamps and a monitor glow, shadows dark but not crushed"
  - "desaturated palette, cold blue-grey with sodium-yellow highlights"
  - "SFX: low room tone, fluorescent hum, rain against the window"
- **11. Avoid:** Handheld wobble. Lens flares. Saturated primaries. Unmotivated drifting or orbiting.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for dialogue, procedure, tech and interface shots, product in a moody set, tension. Worst for sunny lifestyle, big landscape, loose improvisation.

## Wes Anderson

- **1. Signature:** A flat, symmetrical, pastel dollhouse frame with actors facing the lens in deadpan.
- **2. Lens and format:** Wide anamorphic lenses with deep depth of field, so several subjects stay sharp at different depths. A 40mm anamorphic as the default (unverified). Aspect ratio is chosen per period: The Grand Budapest Hotel used 1.37, 1.85 and 2.35 (sources give 2.35 or 2.39 for the widest). Shot on film; Cooke S4 spherical primes from Grand Budapest on.
- **3. Camera:** Tripod and dolly on track. Lateral tracking shots parallel to a wall. Whip pans of exactly 90 or 180 degrees that land on a new composition. Straight push-ins. Top-down insert shots of hands and objects. No handheld, with rare exceptions.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Planimetric: camera square to the back wall. Centre framing, strict left-right symmetry, with pairs or groups balanced. Actors enter and exit along straight lines and stop on marks.
- **5. Light:** Soft, even, high-key. Few hard shadows.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Designed palette of two to four colours per film: pastel pink, mustard, powder blue, red. Colour lives in set and costume, not the grade.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Clockwork. A hold, a precise move, a hold. Action is timed to the camera.
- **8. Performance:** Deadpan, fast flat delivery, direct looks to lens, small precise gestures.
- **9. SFX signature:** Crisp, isolated foley: typewriter keys, a stamp, paper, a bell, shoes on tile. Clean and dry, one sound at a time.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "perfectly symmetrical planimetric composition, camera square to the back wall, subject centred"
  - "40mm-equivalent anamorphic lens, deep focus, everything sharp front to back"
  - "smooth lateral dolly move parallel to the wall at constant 0.5 m/s"
  - "fast 90-degree whip pan that lands exactly on a second symmetrical composition"
  - "soft even high-key light, pastel pink and mustard palette, no harsh shadows"
  - "subject faces the lens, expressionless, moves in straight lines and stops on a mark"
- **11. Avoid:** Handheld. Dutch angles. Shallow-focus bokeh. Gritty, low-key light.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for product tableaux, packaging, food, interiors, title cards, comic beats. Worst for realistic action, grit, raw emotion.

## Wong Kar-wai (with Christopher Doyle)

- **1. Signature:** Smeared, stuttering neon motion around one person who seems to stand still.
- **2. Lens and format:** Two modes. The 1990s mode uses very wide lenses very close to the actor, handheld (one source cites a lens as wide as 6.5mm). The In the Mood for Love mode is formal and detached, with longer lenses shooting through doorways and past foreground objects (longer lenses unverified; Mark Lee Ping-bin finished that film after Doyle left). Spherical, around 1.66–1.85 (unverified). Grainy, pushed film stock.
- **3. Camera:** Handheld and reactive in the wide mode. In the formal mode, slow lateral dolly and slow-motion walk-bys. The signature effect is undercranking combined with step-printing: shoot at a low frame rate, then repeat frames, so motion blurs and stutters while playing at normal speed.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Frames within frames: doorways, corridors, mirrors, rain-streaked glass. Subjects partly hidden, pushed to the edge, or seen from behind.
- **5. Light:** Available and practical light: neon, fluorescent tubes, street lamps, a bare bulb. Mixed colour temperatures left uncorrected.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Saturated reds, greens and ambers. Green fluorescent skin. Deep, rich shadows.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Elastic time. Bursts of smeared speed, then suspended slow motion.
- **8. Performance:** Longing and restraint. Smoking, waiting, looking away. Near-misses instead of contact.
- **9. SFX signature:** Rain, a ticking clock, heels on a stairwell, a noodle-stall sizzle, a jet passing overhead, the hum of a fridge or neon tube.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "step-printed motion: shot at 8 fps with a long shutter, strong motion smear, stuttering cadence"
  - "subject stays sharp and nearly still while the crowd streaks past in blur"
  - "handheld ultra-wide lens very close to the face, slight barrel distortion"
  - "lit only by neon signs and fluorescent tubes, mixed green and red light, wet reflections"
  - "framed through a doorway, subject partly hidden by the frame edge"
- **11. Avoid:** Clean, crisp 24 fps motion. Even white light. Centred, fully visible subjects. Daylight exteriors.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for night city, fashion, mood, romance, music-video energy. Worst for product clarity, legible action, corporate work.

## Quentin Tarantino

- **1. Signature:** A low-angle look up at people staring down into the lens, from inside a car trunk, in rich widescreen.
- **2. Lens and format:** Film, widescreen. 35mm anamorphic 2.39 as the usual format (unverified). The Hateful Eight used 65mm with Ultra Panavision 70 lenses (1.25x squeeze, 2.76:1), with focal lengths from 35mm to 400mm. Wide shots and extreme close-ups, with little in between.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly and crane. The camera is mostly stationary in dialogue. Named moves: trunk POV shot, overhead god's-eye shot, quick crash zoom, long tracking shot following a walk, slow push-in on a talker.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Bold and graphic. Low angles. Extreme close-ups of eyes, hands, feet and objects. Two-shots in profile across a table.
- **5. Light:** Stylised and strong. A hard, hot top light that blooms off tables and shoulders, associated with DP Robert Richardson (unverified).
- **6. Colour and grade:** Saturated primaries: blood red, yellow, black and white. Film-print contrast.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Long dialogue takes with slow-building pressure, then a very fast burst of violence.
- **8. Performance:** Relaxed, talkative, theatrical. Casual behaviour over menace. Long stares before action.
- **9. SFX signature:** Exaggerated foley: loud punches, whooshes, blade rings, heavy gunshots, the crisp sounds of food and drink (exaggeration unverified as a documented practice).
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "low-angle POV from inside a car trunk looking up at two figures staring down into the lens"
  - "anamorphic widescreen 2.39:1, 35mm film, saturated primary colours"
  - "extreme close-up of eyes, then fast crash zoom in"
  - "hard hot top light blooming on the table surface, warm practical lamps behind"
- **11. Avoid:** Handheld shaky-cam. Desaturated grade. Constant camera movement during talk.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for dialogue, standoffs, stylised violence, retro and pop looks, inserts. Worst for delicate naturalism, soft beauty.

## Martin Scorsese

- **1. Signature:** A restless camera that rushes at a face or follows a man through a room as if it were part of his nervous system.
- **2. Lens and format:** Varied, mostly spherical 1.85 with some anamorphic 2.39 (unverified). Wider lenses for moving Steadicam shots, longer for isolated faces.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, Steadicam, crane, handheld. Fast push-in to a face. Whip pan between people (unverified here, widely described). The long Steadicam follow: the Goodfellas club entrance runs about three minutes without a cut. Slow motion at an entrance. Freeze-frame on a decisive beat (unverified here).
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Subjective. Camera at the protagonist's shoulder or sharing his look.
- **5. Light:** Naturalistic base with expressionist accents: red bar light, hard top light on a table, flashbulbs.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Warm skin and wood, deep blacks, strong reds.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Propulsive. Camera movement drives pace.
- **8. Performance:** Volatile, overlapping, improvisational. Charm that tips into threat.
- **9. SFX signature:** Subjective, collaged effects. On Raging Bull the punches were built from animal sounds, smashed melons and slowed or reversed recordings, with flashbulbs layered from several sources. Ambient sound dropping away at a moment of focus (unverified).
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "fast dolly push-in from medium shot to tight close-up in one second, ending in a hard stop"
  - "long Steadicam follow shot behind the subject through a crowded room, shoulder height, continuous"
  - "whip pan from one face to another, motion blur on the pan"
  - "SFX: crowd murmur and clinking glasses that drop away to a single close sound"
- **11. Avoid:** Static, symmetrical tableaux. Cold, clinical palettes. Empty frames.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for character entrances, nightlife, crowds, energy, montage. Worst for minimalism, slow contemplation.

## Steven Spielberg

- **1. Signature:** A slow push-in on a face looking up in wonder, backlit through haze.
- **2. Lens and format:** Wide-leaning. A 21mm on Super 35 is often cited as his eye (secondary source). Stopped down around f/5.6–8 on earlier films so foreground and background both read (single source).
- **3. Camera:** Dolly and crane first, Steadicam, handheld for combat. The "oner": one unobtrusive long take that passes through wide, medium and close-up by moving the camera and the actors together. Push-in on a reaction. Saving Private Ryan was about 90 percent handheld with a narrowed 45 or 90 degree shutter for crisp, staccato motion, and lenses stripped of coatings.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Deep staging. Foreground, middle and background all active. Actors walk into their own close-ups. The reaction is shown before the thing being reacted to.
- **5. Light:** Strong backlight through smoke or haze, giving shafts and haloed edges. Soft fill on faces.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Warm and nostalgic in adventure and family work; bleached and desaturated in war and science fiction.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Choreographed and readable. Every few seconds the frame recomposes into a new shot without a cut.
- **8. Performance:** Open faces. Awe, fear and warmth shown plainly. Natural overlap, especially with children.
- **9. SFX signature:** Sound that precedes sight: a distant thud, rippling water, rustling leaves, an unseen approach. Crisp, punchy impacts.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "slow dolly push-in on the subject's face as they look up past the camera in awe, eyes catching light"
  - "strong backlight through atmospheric haze, visible light shafts, glowing rim on hair and shoulders"
  - "21mm-equivalent wide lens, deep focus, action staged in foreground and background at once"
  - "single continuous take: starts wide, subject walks toward camera into a close-up, camera adjusts gently"
  - "SFX: a low distant thud, then leaves rustling, before anything is seen"
- **11. Avoid:** Flat frontal light. Cold irony or deadpan. Static shots with no blocking.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for wonder, reveals, family and brand warmth, adventure, creature or product reveals. Worst for austere minimalism, deadpan comedy.

## Akira Kurosawa

- **1. Signature:** Figures compressed by a long lens against driving rain or wind, with something moving in every part of the frame.
- **2. Lens and format:** Multiple cameras running at once from the second half of Seven Samurai onward. On Ran, his crew reported that he disliked wide angles and that the zooms were locked off, very often at 75mm. How much telephoto the earlier films used is debated; careful staging explains some of the depth. Academy ratio early, 2.35 widescreen from the late 1950s (unverified). Black and white with deep focus, then bold colour late.
- **3. Camera:** Tripod and dolly. Pans that follow running figures or horses.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Geometric group staging: triangles, lines, individuals against the crowd. Strong diagonals. Every plane has movement: weather, crowd, banners, the lead.
- **5. Light:** Hard sun, high contrast. Heavy rain and fog used as texture.
- **6. Colour and grade:** High-contrast black and white. In colour, saturated primaries coded to groups (red, yellow, blue).
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Stillness, then an explosion of movement, then stillness. Axial cuts jump straight in or out along the lens axis. Wipes between scenes, mostly in the black-and-white period.
- **8. Performance:** Large, physical, readable from far away. Emotion expressed through a single clear gesture.
- **9. SFX signature:** Weather as sound: rain, wind, mud, flapping banners, hooves, arrows. A held silence before a strike.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "75mm-equivalent telephoto, compressed perspective, figures stacked close together in depth"
  - "torrential rain and strong wind, mud, cloth and banners whipping in the wind"
  - "movement in every layer of the frame: foreground weather, midground crowd, background horses"
  - "long stillness, then sudden explosive action in the last second"
- **11. Avoid:** Wide-angle distortion. Still air and static backgrounds. Soft, low-contrast light.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for action, group scenes, weather, epic period work, duels. Worst for intimate modern interiors, small product shots.

## Andrei Tarkovsky

- **1. Signature:** A very long, very slow take in which water, wind or fire moves and almost nothing else does.
- **2. Lens and format:** Standard to moderately long spherical lenses, 1.37 or 1.66 framing (unverified). Mixes colour with black-and-white or sepia stock inside one film, a practice dating from Andrei Rublev.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly and crane. Extremely slow lateral track or push-in, lasting minutes. Slow glide directly above water, soil and submerged objects.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Painterly, with depth. Figures seen from behind or small in a landscape.
- **5. Light:** Natural overcast, mist, a single window. A change of light during the take: a cloud passes, a room brightens.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Muted greens, browns and greys. Monochrome or sepia for one world, soft colour for another.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Duration is the point. Nostalghia has 123 shots in 125 minutes, about a minute each. Events are natural processes: rain starts and stops, a gust crosses a field, something burns.
- **8. Performance:** Inward and quiet. Slow walking, lying down, long looks off-frame.
- **9. SFX signature:** Dripping water, running streams, wind through grass and trees, distant trains, a dog, creaking wood, a fire. Natural sound foregrounded.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "single unbroken take, extremely slow lateral dolly at 0.05 m/s"
  - "rain falling inside a ruined room, puddles on the floor, water dripping from the ceiling"
  - "a gust of wind crosses the tall grass toward camera, then the air settles"
  - "muted greens and browns, overcast soft light, mist in the distance"
  - "SFX: steady dripping water, wind in trees, distant train"
- **11. Avoid:** Cutting. Fast movement. Saturated colour. Clean, dry, modern surfaces.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for landscape, memory, atmosphere, meditative brand films. Worst for action, product clarity, anything under five seconds.

## Ridley Scott

- **1. Signature:** Shafts of hard light cutting through smoke in a densely built, textured world.
- **2. Lens and format:** Widescreen 2.39. Blade Runner is widely described as anamorphic, though one interview complicates that. Gladiator was Super 35 spherical, chosen partly to work with fewer lights. Long lenses for layered, compressed frames; wide for sets. Multiple cameras at once: Gladiator II reportedly ran up to eight.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, crane, Steadicam, handheld in battle. Slow, strong moves through a set.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Layered depth: foreground clutter, mid subject, bright smoky background. Figures in silhouette against a hot background.
- **5. Light:** The Blade Runner recipe: warm soft light from below in the foreground, hard backlight and smoke behind. Light broken up by blinds, fans, grilles and rain (unverified detail). Smoke is required for the beams to be visible.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Cold blue against warm amber. Steel blue for exteriors and battles, gold for interiors.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Atmosphere first. The air moves (smoke, rain, dust, embers) even when the camera is still. Action is energetic and covered from many angles.
- **8. Performance:** Grounded and stoic. Physical, weathered presence.
- **9. SFX signature:** Dense environmental layers: rain, steam vents, machinery hum, distant crowds, fire crackle, metal on metal.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "hard backlight through thick smoke, visible light shafts, subject in near silhouette"
  - "warm soft uplight on the face from below, cold blue background"
  - "densely detailed set, wet surfaces, layered foreground objects out of focus"
  - "SFX: steady rain, hissing steam, low machinery hum"
- **11. Avoid:** Clean air. Flat frontal light. Minimalist empty sets. Pastel colour.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for world-building, science fiction, period epic, cars, perfume and luxury commercials. Worst for light comedy, minimal product on white.

## Paul Thomas Anderson

- **1. Signature:** A warm, grainy film image that stays with one face, or follows one person, for a long time.
- **2. Lens and format:** Film always. The Master was shot more than half on 65mm, framed 1.85, and used the big format for faces rather than vistas. Anamorphic 2.39 on the earlier ensemble films (unverified). Older lenses with flare, softness and falloff (unverified).
- **3. Camera:** Steadicam and dolly. Long follow shots through rooms, slow push-ins that end very tight, whip pans to connect people (unverified here, widely described).
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Faces. Close-ups held past comfort.
- **5. Light:** Naturalistic and warm. Window light, practical lamps, overexposed windows, haze in the room.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Photochemical look. Warm skin, creamy highlights, period-accurate palettes.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Long takes that let a performance build and turn inside the shot.
- **8. Performance:** Big, unpredictable, idiosyncratic. Actors given room to escalate.
- **9. SFX signature:** Intimate, close detail: breathing, fabric, cutlery, a scraping chair, footsteps on wood. Uncomfortable quiet between lines.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "shot on 65mm film, visible grain, warm creamy highlights, slight halation around windows"
  - "50mm-equivalent lens, tight close-up held for the whole shot, shallow depth of field"
  - "very slow dolly push-in, from medium shot to close-up over ten seconds"
  - "soft warm window light, practical lamps, light haze in the room"
  - "SFX: close breathing, fabric rustle, a chair scraping on a wooden floor"
- **11. Avoid:** Digital sharpness. Fast cutting. Teal-orange grade. Symmetry for its own sake.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for portraits, period pieces, character studies, fashion and craft films. Worst for effects action, slick tech.

## Alfonso Cuarón (with Emmanuel Lubezki)

- **1. Signature:** One very long, wide, unbroken shot that moves through chaos as if the camera were a person standing there.
- **2. Lens and format:** Wide spherical lenses with deep focus. An 18mm as the main lens on Children of Men (unverified). Roma, which Cuarón shot himself, used the Alexa 65 with Prime 65 lenses from 24mm to 150mm, stopped to about T4.5–5.6, in digital black and white.
- **3. Camera:** Handheld and Steadicam for the immersive long takes, some of which are several takes joined invisibly. In Roma, tripod and dolly: slow, level pans and lateral tracking at a fixed distance. In Gravity, a drifting, rotating camera with no horizon and an opening shot of 12 or more minutes.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Wide frame, with the subject and their environment given equal weight. Background action is as choreographed as the foreground. The camera wanders off the lead to look at something else, then returns.
- **5. Light:** Natural and available light. Windows and sky. Practicals at night.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Desaturated green-grey for the dystopia. Crisp, wide-tonal-range monochrome for Roma.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Real time. Events overlap and interrupt each other.
- **8. Performance:** Naturalistic, physical, reactive.
- **9. SFX signature:** Immersive, directional ambience: street life, dogs, distant shouts, traffic. Muffled hearing or ear-ring after a blast. Silence in vacuum, with sound carried only through contact.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "single continuous unbroken take, no cuts, camera moves with the subject through the whole scene"
  - "18mm-equivalent wide lens, deep focus, subject and full environment both sharp"
  - "handheld at eye level, follows closely behind, then drifts to look at background action and returns"
  - "natural window light only, desaturated grey-green" (or "crisp black and white with full tonal range")
  - "SFX: layered real-world ambience from all directions, a dog barking in the distance"
- **11. Avoid:** Cutting to close-ups. Shallow focus. Isolating the subject from the setting.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for immersive action, journeys, one-take brand films, environments. Worst for product inserts, graphic design-led frames.

## Terrence Malick

- **1. Signature:** A wide lens floating close around people at sunset, with the sun flaring just behind them.
- **2. Lens and format:** Wide-angle spherical primes used very close to actors, with deep focus. This is one of the working rules he and Lubezki followed from The New World on. Ultra-wide focal lengths around 12–14mm (unverified). 2.39 or 1.85 by film (unverified).
- **3. Camera:** Steadicam and handheld (support unverified in this check, widely described). The camera roams, circles, drifts off a face to hands, grass or sky. Low angles looking up.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Loose and off-centre. People with their backs to camera, walking away, turning. Tilt up to treetops or sky.
- **5. Light:** Natural light only in daytime; existing light in dark interiors. Backlight preferred, which also helps shots from different times cut together. Low sun, window light.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Natural. Warm gold sun, green fields, blue dusk. No heavy grade.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Fragments. The camera catches a gesture in the middle and leaves before it finishes.
- **8. Performance:** Wordless behaviour: touching, running, spinning, looking at the sky.
- **9. SFX signature:** Wind in grass, birdsong, insects, water, distant thunder, children far off. Dialogue treated as a quiet, low-level element.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "golden hour, low sun directly behind the subject, natural lens flare, no artificial light"
  - "14mm-equivalent ultra-wide lens, very close to the subject, deep focus"
  - "floating Steadicam drifts around the subject, slightly low angle, never settles"
  - "subject walks away through tall grass, fingertips brushing the stalks, back to camera"
  - "SFX: wind in grass, birdsong, distant thunder"
- **11. Avoid:** Tripod lock-offs. Studio lighting. Centred, posed framing. Long lenses.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for nature, family, lifestyle, fragrance, emotional montage. Worst for plot, dialogue, hard product detail.

## Bong Joon-ho

- **1. Signature:** Precise, storyboard-exact frames in which the architecture tells you who is above and who is below.
- **2. Lens and format:** Parasite was shot on the Alexa 65 so frames could be wide without resorting to very wide lenses. Analyses list ARRI Prime DNA glass and a 2.39 spherical frame (secondary source). Moderate focal lengths, moderate depth of field.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, crane, Steadicam. Vertical moves that follow characters up and down stairs and slopes. Lateral tracking. Slow push-ins. Every shot is storyboarded before the shoot.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Lines in the set divide the frame and separate characters. Height equals status. Ensemble blocking where several people move in the same shot.
- **5. Light:** Sunlight, or the lack of it, separates worlds: big daylight windows for wealth, dim fluorescent and sodium for the basement. Almost all of Parasite was shot on controllable sets.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Clean and warm for the wealthy space; green-grey and murky for the poor one.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Controlled. A single shot can turn from comic to frightening.
- **8. Performance:** Naturalistic with comic exaggeration at the edges. Sudden changes of tone.
- **9. SFX signature:** Rain and floodwater, footsteps on stairs, a doorbell or intercom, the hum of a house. Ordinary domestic sounds made ominous.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "camera descends with the subject down a long flight of stairs, smooth and steady"
  - "a vertical line in the set splits the frame between the two characters"
  - "bright natural sunlight through a floor-to-ceiling window" (or "dim green fluorescent basement light")
  - "SFX: heavy rain, footsteps on concrete steps, a quiet electrical hum"
- **11. Avoid:** Improvised handheld. Random framing. A uniform lighting scheme across locations.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for interiors, architecture, ensemble scenes, dark comedy, suspense. Worst for abstract mood pieces, open landscape.

## Park Chan-wook

- **1. Signature:** A lush, ornate frame with a flamboyant, perfectly controlled camera move and a sting of violence or desire.
- **2. Lens and format:** Widescreen. Oldboy leaned on longer primes and zooms. The Handmaiden was shot on Alexa with vintage anamorphic glass (sources name Hawk or Angénieux), giving barrel distortion and soft falloff. Zooms used as visible punctuation.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, crane, zoom. The lateral tracking shot along a corridor, held for a whole fight in one take. Shots from behind a character. Fast zoom-ins. Elaborate transitions and match cuts, built in camera where possible.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Frontal and centred, often symmetrical. Patterned wallpaper, screens and mirrors.
- **5. Light:** Stylised but sourced. Rich pools of practical light, strong colour in the set.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Deep greens, purples, reds and gold. Jewel-toned sets. Some analyses describe the grade itself as restrained (secondary source).
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Elegant build, abrupt transition. A long take broken by a sudden fast move.
- **8. Performance:** Heightened and exact. Small gestures weighted with meaning; composed faces.
- **9. SFX signature:** Sensual close foley: scissors, silk, a thimble on teeth, a hammer, breathing. Sharp and tactile.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "lateral side-on tracking shot along a narrow corridor, camera parallel to the wall, single continuous take"
  - "vintage anamorphic lens, slight barrel distortion, soft focus falloff at the edges"
  - "frontal centred composition against ornate patterned wallpaper, deep green and gold"
  - "fast zoom-in from wide shot to the subject's eyes"
  - "SFX: close tactile foley, silk sliding, metal scissors closing"
- **11. Avoid:** Plain or minimalist sets. Loose documentary handheld. Washed-out colour.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for fashion, luxury, thriller, erotic or gothic mood, stylised fights. Worst for casual realism, bright family work.

## Edgar Wright

- **1. Signature:** A rapid series of punchy close-ups with whip pans and crash zooms, every move landing exactly on a beat.
- **2. Lens and format:** Anamorphic. Baby Driver used Panavision anamorphic primes with anamorphic zooms. Shot on 35mm film (unverified). Wider lenses for comedy frames; zooms for snap.
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, crane, Steadicam, car rigs. Whip pans, crash zooms, fast push-ins, quick tilts. Long choreographed Steadicam walks.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Visual comedy inside the frame: people enter and exit from any edge, things pop up, background gags. Tight insert close-ups of hands doing ordinary tasks.
- **5. Light:** Bright, clean, colourful. Saturated practicals.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Saturated primaries, colour-coded characters, red as an accent.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Staccato. Actions land on a rhythmic grid. Very short shots, each with a single action.
- **8. Performance:** Fast, physical, precise comic timing. Deadpan reactions.
- **9. SFX signature:** Hyper-real, percussive foley: whooshes on pans, slams, clicks, zips, gear shifts, door thuds, each sharply synchronised to the action (whoosh convention unverified in this check).
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "fast whip pan with motion blur that lands on a tight close-up"
  - "crash zoom in on the subject's face, 0.3 seconds"
  - "extreme close-up insert of a hand performing one quick action, snappy and exact"
  - "every action lands on an even rhythmic beat, staccato timing"
  - "SFX: sharp whoosh on the pan, hard click, door slam, each exactly on the action"
- **11. Avoid:** Slow, unmotivated drifting. Muddy grade. Long static dialogue frames.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for comedy, product-use inserts, fast montages, car action, social-length cuts. Worst for solemn drama, slow landscape.

## Zack Snyder

- **1. Signature:** A heroic body frozen in extreme slow motion, then snapped back to full speed, in a dark, crushed, painterly frame.
- **2. Lens and format:** Varies. 300 ran several cameras with different focal lengths side by side so the edit could jump in on an impact within one move. Army of the Dead, which he photographed himself, used RED Monstro cameras with vintage Canon 50mm f/0.95 lenses shot wide open, for extremely shallow focus. A 1.33 frame for his director's-cut superhero film (unverified).
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, crane, high-speed. Speed ramping: normal speed to extreme slow motion and back, in one shot. Low angles.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Tableau, like a comic panel or a painting. Hero centred, low angle, cape or cloth in motion, particles in the air.
- **5. Light:** Hard, sculpting, high-contrast. Strong rim light, deep shadow, stormy skies.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Desaturated, with crushed blacks. Bronze and sepia, or steel blue. One selective colour (red) kept vivid. Bleach-bypass style processing (unverified).
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Time stretches at the peak of an action, then snaps.
- **8. Performance:** Statuesque. Poses, clenched jaws, mythic seriousness.
- **9. SFX signature:** Slowed, bass-heavy impacts, whooshes that stretch with the slow motion, cloth snap, metal ring, a boom on the ramp back to speed.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "speed ramp: starts at normal speed, drops to extreme slow motion at 200 fps at the moment of impact, then snaps back to normal speed"
  - "low-angle hero shot, subject centred, cloth billowing, dust and embers hanging in the air"
  - "desaturated bronze palette with one vivid red element"
  - "50mm lens wide open at f/0.95, razor-thin focus, heavy swirling bokeh"
  - "SFX: stretched low whoosh during slow motion, then a deep boom on impact"
- **11. Avoid:** Naturalistic flat light. Bright cheerful colour. Constant real-time motion with no ramp.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for hero moments, sport, action, fantasy, athletic product. Worst for dialogue, naturalism, comedy.

## Michael Bay

- **1. Signature:** A low camera circling a hero who rises into frame against a blazing sky, with everything in the shot moving.
- **2. Lens and format:** Anamorphic widescreen with flares (unverified). Long telephoto lenses for stacked, compressed layers, mixed with very wide low-angle shots (unverified). Many cameras rolling at once (unverified).
- **3. Camera:** Dolly, crane, helicopter, car rigs. The 360-degree hero shot: the camera circles a subject, usually from below, often in slow motion, as they stand or turn. Analysis by Tony Zhou breaks the style into scale, parallax and motion: camera, background and actors all move, often in opposing directions, while time is stretched.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Layers. Foreground objects sweep past; background has vehicles, aircraft, flags, sparks. Low angle makes people monumental. Depth exaggerated by parallax.
- **5. Light:** Golden-hour backlight, hot rim, glossy highlights on skin and metal (unverified as a stated method).
- **6. Colour and grade:** Highly saturated with heightened orange. Teal shadows against orange skin (teal-orange pairing unverified). High contrast and gloss.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Very short shots, each with maximum internal motion. Slow motion on the hero beat.
- **8. Performance:** Iconic poses. Looking up and past camera, sweat, determination.
- **9. SFX signature:** Layered explosions, metallic clanks and servo whirs, jet and helicopter passes, tyre squeal, debris patter.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "low-angle camera circles the subject 180 degrees as they rise to standing, slow motion"
  - "telephoto lens, compressed background, layers of movement: helicopters, dust, sparks behind the subject"
  - "foreground objects sweep past the lens for strong parallax"
  - "golden-hour backlight, hot rim light, anamorphic horizontal lens flare"
  - "highly saturated, glossy, strong orange highlights against teal shadows"
  - "SFX: helicopter pass overhead, deep explosion in the distance, debris falling"
- **11. Avoid:** Static eye-level frames. Muted colour. Empty backgrounds. Long quiet holds.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for cars, military and industrial hardware, sport, hero product shots, high-energy commercials. Worst for intimate dialogue, subtle emotion.

## Spike Jonze (commercial and advert extra)

- **1. Signature:** A warm, soft, slightly awkward human moment that turns surreal without the camera making a fuss.
- **2. Lens and format:** On Her, with Hoyte van Hoytema: ARRI Alexa, fast and vintage lenses, minimal lighting, a "pristine and clean" brief. One account credits uncoated lenses for the soft warm flares. Shallow depth of field on close-ups (unverified).
- **3. Camera:** Handheld or gentle Steadicam that follows behaviour (support unverified in this check). Simple, observational moves. In the advert work, an ordinary setting filmed plainly while something impossible or choreographed happens in it (unverified).
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Close on faces. Soft foregrounds. A single person alone in a city or a flat. Performance leads; the frame follows.
- **5. Light:** Soft and low. Window light, practicals, city glow. Lit as little as possible.
- **6. Colour and grade:** Warm reds, oranges, pinks and pastels. Blue deliberately drained from the image.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Lets a moment play out. The surprise arrives mid-shot, without a cut announcing it.
- **8. Performance:** Sincere, vulnerable, sometimes suddenly physical (a burst into dance). Real, unforced emotion.
- **9. SFX signature:** Quiet domestic realism: room tone, soft city hum, a voice close to the ear, footsteps, breath. Surreal events sound physically real.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "warm palette of reds, corals and soft pinks, no blue anywhere in the frame"
  - "an ordinary apartment filmed plainly while one surreal thing happens, treated as normal"
  - "subject suddenly breaks into an uninhibited physical movement, camera simply follows"
  - "SFX: quiet room tone, soft distant city hum, close breathing"
- **11. Avoid:** Cold blue technology look. Aggressive camera tricks. Ironic distance.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for brand films with heart, tech made human, dance and concept adverts. Worst for hard action, cold luxury.

## Emmanuel Lubezki, natural-light approach (DP style extra)

- **1. Signature:** An ultra-wide lens inches from a face, in real low sun, with the whole landscape sharp behind.
- **2. Lens and format:** Wide primes close to the subject. On The Revenant, mostly a 24mm on the Alexa 65, which he compared to a 12mm on 35mm. Deep focus: skin texture in the foreground and leaves in the far background both sharp. Spherical, clean glass.
- **3. Camera:** Steadicam, handheld, crane, often blended in one long take (blend unverified in this check). The camera moves through the scene like a participant: closes to inches from a face, swings away to the horizon, comes back.
- **4. Composition and blocking:** Close-up and landscape in the same frame. Low angles to the sky and treetops. Actors move around the lens; the lens moves around them.
- **5. Light:** Natural light only. The Revenant used no artificial light. Backlight preferred. Low sun, overcast, dusk, firelight.
- **6. Colour and grade:** True to the location. Cold blue-grey winter; warm gold sun. Wide dynamic range, restrained grade.
- **7. Rhythm inside a shot:** Long, continuous, breathing. The shot's energy rises and falls without a cut.
- **8. Performance:** Physical and exposed. Effort, cold and breath are visible.
- **9. SFX signature:** Close breathing, wind, snow crunch, water, fire crackle, creaking trees. Presence of the environment over everything.
- **10. Prompt translation:**
  - "12mm-equivalent ultra-wide lens, 30 cm from the subject's face, deep focus, landscape sharp behind"
  - "natural light only, low sun behind the subject, no artificial lighting, no fill"
  - "one continuous take: camera drifts from a close-up of the face up to the treetops and back"
  - "overcast cold daylight, blue-grey palette, visible breath in the air"
  - "SFX: close breathing, wind through bare trees, footsteps in snow"
- **11. Avoid:** Studio key lights. Telephoto compression. Shallow-focus beauty close-ups. Midday flat sun from the front.
- **12. Best for / worst for:** Best for outdoor, adventure, automotive in landscape, documentary-feel brand work. Worst for controlled studio product, night interiors that need shaping.

## Blend rules

- **Treat style as five independent axes:** lens and format, camera movement, light, colour, and rhythm. Take each axis from one director only. Two sources for the same axis is where contradictions come from.
- **Lens and colour mix freely.** A wide symmetrical one-point frame (Kubrick) takes a warm no-blue palette (Jonze) or a pastel one (Anderson) without conflict.
- **Movement and rhythm must come from the same family.** Slow straight glides belong with long holds. Whip pans and crash zooms belong with staccato timing. Do not ask for "extremely slow dolly" and "snappy rhythmic action" in one shot.
- **Light sets the ceiling for colour.** Natural-light approaches (Malick, Lubezki) cannot carry heavy designed palettes; use location colour. Stylised light (Scott, Snyder) can carry a strong grade.
- **Depth of field follows the lens choice, not the mood.** Wide and close means deep focus. If you want razor-thin focus, switch to a 50–85mm-equivalent wide open. Do not ask for "14mm, shallow depth of field".
- **Pick one support.** Handheld, Steadicam, dolly and locked-off tripod are mutually exclusive inside a single clip. Write one.
- **Symmetry excludes roaming.** Centre-framed planimetric composition (Anderson, Kubrick, Park) breaks as soon as the camera orbits or drifts. Combine it only with straight push, pull or lateral moves.
- **Time effects do not stack.** Use step-printing, or speed ramping, or straight slow motion. One per shot.
- **Atmosphere is the safest thing to borrow.** Haze and backlight (Scott, Spielberg), weather in every layer (Kurosawa), or wind and water (Tarkovsky) can be added to almost any other director's lens and movement.
- **SFX follows rhythm, not lens.** Quiet room tone and single sounds go with long takes. Percussive, synchronised foley goes with fast cutting. Borrow the sound from whoever you borrowed the rhythm from.
